Market Research Methodology Toolbox

The instruments behind the Continuous Discovery System. Clients see one living view; these four methods are how we feed it. Three of them tell us where to look and how big something is. One of them tells us what is actually true.

Internal page. This is intentionally unlinked from the client demo and never shown to clients. The four methods below do not appear in the client-facing Continuous Discovery System, by design. They sit on the supply side of our process, the workshop behind the storefront.
01

How the four split

One gradient organises everything: stated / attitudinal signals are weak (good for orienting, never proof), and observed past behaviour is strong (it can validate). It is the same logic that makes the client demo treat social listening as a weak pre-frame signal. Three of these instruments are breadth / sizing / discovery. One is the depth-and-evidence engine.

The rule we enforce

A big survey number is still an assumption about demand, not proof anyone will hire us. Only the interviews, disciplined to real past behaviour, are allowed to push a node from assumption → emerging → validated in the client view. The surveys and the Tribe Map decide what and who we interview, and how we prioritise what we find.

Instrument 1 · the evidence engine

AI-moderated Video Interviews

Continuous, AI-moderated qualitative interviews, run async and at volume. This is the one instrument that touches the evidence ladder, and it is what makes "continuous discovery" literally continuous.

Strong · can validate Loop 1 · ProgressLoop 4 · CausalLoop 5 · Delivery
What it is

An AI moderator runs JTBD interviews over video, on the participant's own time. It probes the timeline and forces of a real, recent switch, the same script a human researcher would run, freed from a single moderator's calendar.

Because it is async and parallel, the demand spine runs at volume instead of trickling in one booked call at a time.

What it yields
  • First-hand past-switch stories in the customer's words, with quotes captured as evidence.
  • Struggling moments and forces, ready to roll up into the Job and the opportunity space.
  • Volume, so the Job can be confirmed across many interviews, not a handful.
→

Where it plugs into the client system

It is the engine of Loop 1 · Progress. In the client demo that loop has a "Schedule a JTBD interview" button bottlenecked on a human; swap it for AI-moderated video and the loop runs continuously. The same instrument is reused to probe assumptions in Loop 4 · Causal Learning and to capture reactions to a live increment in Loop 5 · Delivery & Impact, and to re-interview on the measure-impact → re-prioritise loop.

Guardrail. Evidence-grade only if the script holds the line: real, recent past behaviour, never hypotheticals or feature wishlists. AI moderation must not lead the witness. Same discipline as a good human interviewer, just at scale.
Instrument 2 · sizing

Market Potential Index survey

A quantitative survey that sizes and ranks demand across segments and opportunities. Breadth to complement the depth of the interviews: it answers "how big," it never answers "why."

Moderate · sizes, doesn't prove Define · FoundationLoop 3 · Opportunity
What it is

A structured survey that scores the potential of each segment and struggling moment, frequency, intensity, willingness, reach, into a comparable index. Quant breadth across the market, fielded fast.

What it yields
  • A sizing layer on the demand landscape, to sharpen the opening frame.
  • A ranking of opportunities, which struggling moment is worth most.
  • Where to point the interviews first, so depth follows the biggest prizes.
→

Where it plugs into the client system

Two touch-points. At Define it sizes the demand landscape and helps sharpen the framing assumption, a stronger cousin of the External-context card, still on the supply side. Then it reappears in Loop 3 · Opportunity to rank the struggling moments that interviews surface, so prioritisation is not a hunch.

Guardrail. A high index is still stated preference, an assumption about demand, not proof of behaviour. It sizes and ranks; it never moves a node to validated on its own. Causality stays the interviews' job.
Instrument 3 · discovery

Consumer Directed Insight survey

A consumer-led survey where respondents direct what matters, rather than answering a fixed questionnaire. A bridge between quant breadth and qual depth: it surfaces candidate struggles and the customer's own language, at scale.

Emerging · hypotheses to test Define · External contextLoop 1 · ProgressLoop 3 · Opportunity
What it is

An open, consumer-directed instrument: respondents tell us what they care about and in what words, instead of rating our pre-set options. It captures the demand side's own framing across a wide sample.

What it yields
  • Candidate Jobs and struggles we had not pre-imagined, before we book interviews.
  • The customer's vocabulary, raw material for the "in their words" requirement.
  • A shortlist of who and what to interview, so depth is well aimed.
→

Where it plugs into the client system

It sits right next to External Context in Define, then feeds Loop 1 · Progress with candidate Jobs to probe and Loop 3 · Opportunity with moments in the consumer's own words. It is discovery scaffolding before and between the interviews.

Guardrail. Still a stated signal, consumer-led but not observed. It generates hypotheses; it does not confirm them. Everything it surfaces enters as an assumption to be tested in the interviews.
Instrument 4 · the lens

Tribe Map

A segmentation by tribes, shared identity, values and belonging, rather than demographics. It maps the social landscape the product could live in, and doubles as a recruiting map for the interviews.

Lens · not an evidence-mover Define · Who we're studyingSocial job (JTBD)
What it is

A map of the tribes a market is made of, communities bound by identity, values and belonging, and where they congregate. Not "35-44, urban," but "the kind of person who looks after themselves."

What it yields
  • The social dimension of the Job, the identity people are reaching for.
  • A recruiting / sampling map: which tribes to pull interviewees from.
  • The legitimate source of social signals, where listening should point.
→

Where it plugs into the client system

It lives in Define · Who we're studying. The client demo already frames segments by shared Job and carries a Social tag; the Tribe Map is what populates that social layer, and it tells the interview engine which tribes to recruit from. It shapes who and where, upstream of the evidence.

Guardrail. A lens, not a measurement. It organises and recruits; it never sets a node's evidence state. The Job it points at is still an assumption until interviews earn it.
